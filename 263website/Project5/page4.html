Present

the only moment that truly belongs to us—where everything we have been and everything we hope to be collide

Transitioning back to my old life after Spain felt like a breath of fresh air, yet I found myself missing it more as time went on. My senior year of high school was the first "normal" year I'd had in a while, and it became a whirlwind of unforgettable moments—football games, sports teams, prom, and finally walking across that stage at graduation. I had so much gratitude for these experiences, as if I was savoring every moment of the "high school dream" that I hadn't fully experienced until then. The summer passed in the blink of an eye, and before I knew it, I was packing up my life once again—this time for Syracuse University. While I thought I was prepared for the change, the transition was still challenging. I made friends, but I felt a strong pull to spend time alone, grounding myself in this new environment and finding comfort in the routines I began to create. Even though I was surrounded by new people, I found it difficult to put myself out there and embrace all the new experiences. I didn't know what I wanted to study or who I wanted to become, and that uncertainty made everything feel like a big question mark. But then, winter break arrived, and something about being home with friends, family, and, of course, my dog, made me feel like I had hit the reset button. It was over this break that I decided to join a sorority—something I never would have imagined for myself, but it felt like a great opportunity to meet new people and connect with older girls who could offer guidance. My roommate and I both joined Kappa Kappa Gamma, and while we stayed close, we also embraced our own paths within the sorority. It was in the spring semester that things started to click. I changed my major, joined new organizations, and met my "big," Claudia, who helped shift my mindset about college. She reminded me that I wasn't competing with anyone—I was on my own path, with my own timeline. Everything would work out, as long as I kept moving forward. By the time summer came around, I looked back on my first year with immense gratitude. It had been full of challenges, but it had also been full of growth. I had faced my fears and embraced discomfort, and in return, I had learned more about myself than I could have imagined. College wasn't just about academics—it was about connections, memories, and pushing yourself to step outside your comfort zone. The summer offered a refreshing break as I traveled with my best friend, fulfilling a dream we had both shared for years. We'd saved for months, and finally, we were off on a trip that was all our own. We wandered through the streets of a beautiful city, soaking up the local culture and history, conversing with locals, and exploring off-the-beaten-path destinations. There is nothing more rewarding to me than learning directly from those who know a place best. The rest of the summer was spent working, hanging out with friends, and oddly missing school. As I prepared for my sophomore year, I made a new set of goals for myself. I've found that setting goals helps me stay balanced between my studies, my hobbies, and my social life. During the fall semester, I built new friendships, visited my cousins in New York City, and dove into coding projects in my classes at the iSchool. I also went through sorority recruitment again, but this time, I was on the other side, as a seasoned member. It felt like a full circle moment, and I couldn't wait to help guide the new members and share the lessons I had learned. Now, I have adopted two new babies into our sorority family, and I now get to serve as a role model for them both in the same way that Claudia was for me. Looking back on this journey, I can't help but feel proud of how far I've come. While I still have so much to learn and discover, I now know that growth happens in unexpected places, and I'm excited to continue embracing each new chapter, no matter how challenging it may seem. I may not have everything figured out, but I've learned that it's okay to not have all the answers—what matters is that I'm still learning, still growing, and still moving forward.
