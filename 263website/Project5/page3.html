Adolescence

the bridge between who we were and who we are becoming.

Suddenly, I was in high school, and my world shifted dramatically as I moved into boarding school—by far the most significant change I had ever experienced up until that point. Being away from home was hard, but once I found my circle of friends, I started to embrace the newness of it all. When I think back to that time, I don't even cringe (though I'm not entirely sure what I was thinking with some of my style choices). What stands out the most from those years is how I learned the value of loyalty and the kind of friendships that would help me weather life's storms. Then, of course, COVID hit, and my world turned upside down once again. I moved back home for a while, but before long, I found myself back at boarding school for my sophomore year. These days were marked by isolation, wellness surveys, and the strictest of rules for socializing. Yet, despite everything, we found a way to make the most of it. I forged new friendships, although the group of girls I spent the most time with was very cliquey. We had our moments of fun, but being in that group made me realize something important: while I cherished these friendships, I didn't want to be boxed into just one set of people. I was craving new connections, new perspectives. I was learning so much about myself, and I wanted a challenge. And so, in a whirlwind of chaos and rapid changes, I made one of the boldest decisions of my life: I applied for a year-abroad program in Zaragoza, Spain—despite knowing virtually no Spanish beyond the basics of a level 1 class. That summer, I made a new friend group, and saying goodbye to them at the end of the summer was incredibly tough. I was terrified, filled with doubt about what lay ahead. When I first arrived in Spain, I was stunned by the beauty of the city and the rich history that surrounded me. I explored as much as I could, made a few friends, and marveled at how far I had come. But that first experience wasn't easy. My host family and I struggled to communicate, and I spent much of my time feeling alone and unsure. Things got better when I stayed with a friend's host family during the winter break, and I began to feel a deep sense of gratitude for their kindness. The mom made me a handmade gift, and it was in those small, thoughtful moments that I began to appreciate just how much kindness could change a person's experience. I quickly realized I needed a better host family situation, and with that realization came one of the toughest moments of my trip: confronting my first host family and telling them I wanted to switch. I've always hated confrontation, so this wasn't easy. But once I made the switch, my life improved tenfold. My new host family—Guillermo, Isabel, and their two adorable children, Daniel and Celia—welcomed me with open arms. In the three short months I spent with them, we traveled together, and they helped me with my Spanish. I became incredibly close with them. The more I embraced this new life, the more I felt myself growing. I also threw myself into school activities, traveling across Spain, and bonding with my classmates. Though there were plenty of homesick moments, by the end of my time there, I was heartbroken to leave. Through my time in Spain, I learned how to navigate difficult situations with resilience, how to leave behind what no longer served me, and most importantly, how to find hope even in tough circumstances. I learned that kindness, even in small gestures, can have an enormous impact on others. And perhaps most of all, I learned that the only way to grow is to push yourself out of your comfort zone, even when you're terrified. Looking back, I see how all of these experiences, these moments of growth, have shaped me into who I am today. From the high school halls to the cobblestone streets of Zaragoza, every part of my journey has been a lesson in perseverance, friendship, and self-discovery. The road ahead will no doubt be full of new challenges, but now I know—whatever comes next, I'll be ready for it.
